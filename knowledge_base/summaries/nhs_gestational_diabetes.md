## en
The NHS page explains that gestational diabetes is high blood sugar that develops during pregnancy and usually goes away after the birth. It happens when the body cannot make enough insulin, the hormone that controls blood sugar, to meet the extra needs of pregnancy, and it is more common in the second or third trimester. It usually causes no symptoms and is found by screening. The risk is higher with a BMI above 30, a previous baby of 4.5 kg or more, gestational diabetes before, a parent or sibling with diabetes, or a South Asian, Black, African-Caribbean or Middle Eastern family origin. Afterwards the risk of type 2 diabetes is higher, so a blood test is advised 6 to 13 weeks after the birth and once a year after that if it is normal.
