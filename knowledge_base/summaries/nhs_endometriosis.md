## en
The NHS page explains that endometriosis is where cells similar to the lining of the womb grow in other places, most often around the womb, ovaries, fallopian tubes and the lining of the pelvis. Symptoms happen when these patches break down and bleed but the blood cannot leave the body: severe period pain that stops normal activities, heavy periods, pelvic pain, pain when pooing or peeing or during sex, extreme tiredness, and sometimes difficulty getting pregnant. Its cause is not known and it can take a long time to recognise, because the symptoms look like other conditions. A doctor may examine you and arrange an ultrasound or MRI scan, and a keyhole operation (laparoscopy) can confirm it. There is no cure, but treatment can help manage symptoms such as pain.
